# Creative direction: what Papa Russ and Dylan want

These are the preferences behind the Polar Express / Hot Chocolate show, gathered over
four versions of feedback. Treat them as defaults for every show. The user's own
direction for a song always wins.

## How to work

1. **Plan before building.** Write a beat sheet first: one block per scene with the
   time, what the music does, the story beat, and what each prop group does (tree,
   trim, snowflakes, beat-keepers, other lights). Share it, let the user mark it up, then
   build. A doc or markdown file works; keep it as the source of truth for later passes.
2. **Change a few things per pass.** The user reviews a version, picks two or three
   areas, and you fix those. Don't redesign everything each time.
3. **Review in the viewer.** Build with `--version` and `--scenes` so notes left with
   the N key say where they are, and read the notes back next pass (SKILL.md Workflow 4).
4. **Give the whole show a story** (for the medley: a train ride that leaves the
   station, speeds up, slows into the station, cocoa, fireworks, goodnight). Every
   choice follows it: speeds, colours, what the tree shows.

## Motion

- **Purpose, not random flashing.** Every movement should mean something: wheels
  turning, tracks passing, snow falling, steam rising. Flashing, strobing and random
  pulsing are only for moments whose energy demands it (the peak, a lyric hit).
- **Flow between looks.** Crossfade (alternating rows, about a bar of overlap) instead
  of cutting. Jarring tree transitions were the first thing called out.
- **Things move together.** Snowflakes in sync: the same spoke lit on every flake, on a
  song-wide phase, even when they join at different times. Rooflines travel in the same
  physical direction across the house, never toward each other. Line props run along
  their wiring, so pick the direction word per line (SKILL.md, plan_format.md).
- **Speed tells the story.** Wheels and trim crawl, chug, go faster, hit full steam,
  then slow to a stop in the dialogue section and start again. Speed changes are smooth
  (pieces that each move whole groups).
- **Don't keep one trick on all show.** The wheel spin was great, but on the whole time
  it got stale. Use it for the train parts, faster each time it returns, and other
  patterns in between: one flake at a time across the house, back and forth, center out,
  star flips, propellers.

## The house trim

- The outline is always involved and acting together, not chopped into sections that
  blink on and off.
- Favourite: **train tracks**, groups of about 4 lights with dark gaps sliding along
  every line at the show's speed. Big moments change the groups' colour (they turn red
  on "hot hot") or jolt them forward, rather than flashing.
- Not too fast and not too busy: high-speed blended bars everywhere read as "too much
  chasing".
- Verticals can react to sound: level meters that follow the whistle's loudness.

## Beat-keepers

Give regular (non-pixel) props a job instead of just on or off:
- A white tree of strands spirals strand by strand on the beat, speeds up to eighths in
  bigger parts, and flashes fully on the big hits.
- Bushes alternate left and right on the beat.
- Both speed up (beat, then eighths, then sixteenths) in builds and flash together on
  punches.

## Quiet sections

A dialogue or lull is never dark. Take lights away and slow everything down (about 60%
brightness, slow patterns, one prop at a time), with something small that changes, like
the train slowing to a stop. Near-blackout read as "too dark and too long".

## Colour

- Default to traditional Christmas colours: red, green, white, and light blue for
  snow and cold scenes.
- No neon pink, purple or acid yellow.
- Gold, orange and warm white are for tree pictures where they sell the image
  (firebox, cocoa, a gold bell, lamplight), not for the trim and other props.
- Ask about warm white versus pure white if the user hasn't said.
